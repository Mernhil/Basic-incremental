Weapon tier looks

How one weapon family evolves from Bronze to Silver, Gold, Prismatic and Unique, like a single Brave Frontier unit evolving (DESIGN.md section 5, Tier). Five options, each shown on the sword ladder and then on a staff, a bow and a maul. Drafts only: nothing here is in the game, and the Unique step is always the existing Flameblade or Rootbloom Staff.

Every sprite is drawn on the card item grid (100 x 120). Each picture is shown at 2x (the full-screen card scale), at 1x (the native pixels) and inside its tier frame at 1x and 2x. The card mock is an approximation of the real card layout: the shipped frame sprite, a dark body and the item at its true size.

Art cost

About 25 SE: 12 hand-drawn bodies plus 36 re-forges (new ramp and 10 to 30 px of ornament each). No new silhouettes.

Readable at card size

The silhouette never changes, so the family is recognised at once at 1x. The tier shows as a metal colour, which holds at 1x. Bronze and Gold are the closest pair (both warm); the red grip, the gems and the sparkles separate them.

Cel-shaded style

It is the one metal ladder of DESIGN.md section 12 (bronze, silver, gold, prismatic) applied to the weapon, the same language as frames, coins and medals. Five-tone cel ramps, one ink outline.

With rarity

Layers well. Rarity keeps the parts (winged guard, ribbon, runes) and its accent colour; tier takes the blade or head metal. Caveat: a Silver tier Common and a Rare card both read as blue-grey steel.

Weak point

A Silver sword is not visibly stronger than a Bronze one, only richer. The jump from Prismatic crystal to the Unique flame has no ramp.

Art cost

About 41 SE: every tier is a new silhouette (a new body at Bronze, then 0.8 SE each for the next three) across 12 weapons. The most expensive option.

Readable at card size

Strongest at 1x: size and silhouette tell the tier in one flat colour. But Bronze fills about 55% of the card window and looks small, and Prismatic fills the whole 100 x 120 box, leaving no room for the aura and sparkles that Gold and above already get.

Cel-shaded style

The closest to a Brave Frontier unit evolving: the thing itself grows. Cel style is intact. Material stays one steel, so it does not use the metal ladder.

With rarity

Collides. Rarity already adds a winged guard at Epic and an ornate shape at Legendary, so a Gold tier Common would look like an Epic card. It needs a rule: tier owns size, rarity owns ornament.

Weak point

Every weapon needs its own growth plan, and bows, shields and gauntlets grow awkwardly (a bigger shield stops fitting the window).

Art cost

About 3 SE of new art: the 12 Common bodies already exist and the effects are generated from the outline by the existing fx loop. New work is the Silver warm-up, the rainbow shell and per-weapon tuning.

Readable at card size

Best for recognising the weapon, since the picture is identical at every tier. The tier reads as a glow, which works at 1x on a dark window, but Silver is only 1 to 2 px and light windows (Common) eat the contrast. Effects must stay calmer than hits.

Cel-shaded style

Exactly what section 12 already says (effects from the item's own shape, none at Bronze and Silver, from Gold, stronger at Prismatic). Hard pixels only in the drafts; in game they are the existing live loop.

With rarity

Orthogonal, which is its best point: rarity picks the body, tier picks the aura, so no clash and no matrix of looks.

Weak point

It is an effect upgrade, not a new look. Section 5 asks for a new look within the family, and a still screenshot of Bronze and Gold (effects frozen) shows the same sword.

Rare or better cardsThey start higher on the ladder and are capped at Void; a Legendary card at Prismatic would show the Void sprite.

Art cost

0 SE: already drawn; only wiring. It is the cost of not deciding.

Readable at card size

These are approved sprites and read fine on their own. But Bronze and Silver differ only by the guard colour, which is hard to see at card size, and the tier is not told by shape until Epic.

Cel-shaded style

Fully consistent: it is the approved art. It is the only option here that is not new work.

With rarity

Collides head on: tier and rarity are the same axis. A Common card at Silver is indistinguishable from a Rare card, and a Mythic card has almost nowhere to go.

Weak point

Players cannot tell 'this card is rare' from 'this card is upgraded'; that is the reason section 5 calls it temporary.

Art cost

About 11 SE: reuse the Common body, add 12 anchor points, one shared kit of 4 crest and 6 gem decals, and 48 trim palette swaps. The trim must be a separate part of each baked sprite.

Readable at card size

The crest changes the silhouette at Silver (wings) and Gold (crown), so it reads at 1x. The gems are 4 to 5 px dots and do not read at 1x; they pay off at 2x. The blade stays plain, so the weapon stays recognisable.

Cel-shaded style

One visual language for the whole card: wings mean Silver, a crown means Gold, on the frame and the weapon. Cel style fits. It also tells the tier twice, since the frame already does.

With rarity

Layers, with one rule needed: rarity colours the guard today (blue, purple, gold, pink), and tier trim would take that over. Let tier own the metal and rarity own the gem and ribbon colour.

Weak point

The weapon never feels more powerful, only more decorated, and 12 weapons end up with similar-looking crests.

Art cost, side by side

SE (sprite-equivalent): one new hand-shaded 100 x 120 weapon from scratch is 1 SE; a recolour plus 10 to 30 px of ornament is about 0.35; a palette swap is 0.1; an effect generated from the outline costs a one-time 0.5 per effect type. These are estimates from how long each draft took to build, not measurements.

Method and gaps

  • The four new ladders (A, B, C, E) were drawn with a small hard-pixel kit (pix.py, weapons.py, options.py, fx.py): polygons rasterised on the 100 x 120 grid with no anti-aliasing, five-tone ramps lit from the top-left, an ink outline, a light rim and plus sparkles, as the shipped item sprites do. They are seeded and deterministic; build.py regenerates everything.
  • Option D, the unique steps, and the frames are the game's own approved sprites, copied unchanged into existing/ for comparison. They are not part of the checks below (the approved Rootbloom Staff uses 103 colours).
  • pixel_check.py on the 64 new sprites: 0 errors and no orphan pixels. The only warnings are the outer aura pixels of option C, which sit outside the ink outline on purpose (an effect, not an outline gap).
  • Option C is shown as hard-pixel stills and 6-frame loops of what the live effect loop would draw; in the game the effects stay a live canvas behind and in front of the card sprite, so no new baked art would be needed for them.
  • Not done: the bow, maul and staff Unique steps (no unique is drawn for them yet), the other eight weapons (they are described in the tables, not drawn), a motion preview of E's sparks, and a check in the real card (nothing was merged or run in the game).
